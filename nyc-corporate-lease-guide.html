Corporate leases in NYC

Relocating for work or arranging housing for an employee? Start with who signs, who lives there and what the agreement needs to cover.

Start here

What is a corporate lease?

For a residential apartment, a corporate lease generally means a company is named as the tenant and signs the lease to house an employee or other approved occupant. The company takes on the obligations stated in the agreement; the people living there must also be identified and approved as required.

It is a housing arrangement, not an office lease. Putting a company’s name on an apartment lease does not, by itself, permit business operations, rotating guests or short-term rentals.

The first question I ask is simple: who will sign the lease, who will live there, and who will pay? Those answers guide the search.

Choose the structure

Company lease, housing allowance or guaranty?

The company signs
The business applies as the proposed tenant. Confirm the exact legal entity, authorized signer, approved occupants and any separate guaranty.
The employee signs
The individual remains the tenant even if the employer pays the rent or reimburses it through a housing allowance. Payment support alone does not replace the tenant’s obligations.
The employer guarantees
The employee signs the lease and the company gives a separate, landlord-approved guaranty. Its scope depends on the written terms.

“Corporate housing” may instead describe a furnished accommodation service. Read the actual agreement to understand the operator, contracting party, services and cancellation terms. The label alone does not answer those questions.

Source: New York Attorney General’s explanation of lease terms.

The process

How to arrange a corporate lease in NYC.

  1. Set the brief. Agree with HR or your relocation team on the budget, dates, location, furnishings, occupants and proposed leaseholder. Include utilities and other recurring costs in the budget.
  2. Confirm acceptance before touring. Ask whether the owner and building permit the proposed corporate arrangement, lease length and use. For a condo or co-op, confirm management’s application and approval process as well.
  3. Get the application requirements in writing. A corporate application may differ from an individual one. Establish who supplies the company information and who handles the occupant’s paperwork.
  4. Review the agreement. Have the appropriate company signatory and, where needed, your attorney review liability, occupancy, renewal and early-exit terms before signing.
  5. Coordinate the move. Confirm approval, payment instructions, insurance requirements, move-in reservations, utilities and key collection before arranging delivery.

As a local example, UDR’s 10 Hanover Square publishes a separate company application process and business-credit requirements. That is one building’s policy, not a universal NYC standard.

Source: 10 Hanover Square’s corporate-housing FAQ.

A short preparation checklist

Ask management which items apply to your arrangement:

  • Company legal name, registered address and formation information.
  • Evidence that the proposed signer can bind the company.
  • Company financial information, business-credit information or references requested for review.
  • Approved occupant details and the building’s individual application requirements.
  • Any proposed guaranty, insurance documentation and the company contact responsible for payments.

This is a preparation list, not a fixed application standard. A company lease does not automatically remove review of the individual occupant.

Before you sign

Plan for the assignment changing.

A work assignment can end before the lease does. Build that possibility into the conversation while terms are still being negotiated.

Who can live there?

Confirm named occupants and the written process for any replacement employee. Do not assume the company can rotate staff freely.

What if plans change?

Review notice, renewal, early termination, assignment and subletting provisions. An employer transfer is not a promise of a penalty-free exit.

Who owes what?

Identify rent, utilities, damage responsibility, insurance and any guaranty. Employees should understand any separate agreement with their employer.

What is included?

For furnished housing, confirm the inventory, internet, utilities, cleaning and move-out arrangements in writing.

These are discussion points for your lease review. If you need an attorney introduction, I can help connect you.

Budget clearly

What does a corporate lease cost?

There is no single NYC corporate-lease price or standard premium. Compare the actual rent, lease term, furnishings, included services and itemized charges. Some operators publish corporate premiums, but their policies are not a rule for every building.

Source: UDR’s property-specific terms.

Deposits, application charges and agent fees

Deposits: New York generally caps a covered residential deposit or advance at one month’s rent, subject to statutory exceptions. A company lease should not be treated as an automatic exemption. Have the proposed structure and charges reviewed.

Source: New York General Obligations Law § 7-108.

Application charges: the Attorney General describes a $20 limit for residential credit and background checks. Company applications and condo or co-op charges can raise different questions; ask for an itemized schedule and the basis for each charge rather than assuming a quoted fee is permitted.

Source: New York Attorney General’s tenant guide.

Agent fees: NYC’s FARE Act prohibits a landlord’s agent from charging its broker fee to the prospective tenant. A renter can choose to hire and pay their own agent. Confirm representation and any agreed fee before engaging one.

Source: NYC’s FARE Act guidance.

Length of stay

A corporate lease is not a short-term rental workaround.

NYC generally prohibits renting an entire ordinary apartment or home for fewer than 30 days. Calling the booking “corporate housing” does not create an exception. For a shorter assignment, look at lawful hotel or extended-stay accommodation.

A stay of 30 days or more still needs to comply with the lease, building rules and permitted use. Duration alone does not establish that a particular arrangement is allowed.

Source: NYC Office of Special Enforcement.

A note on screening

Company and occupant review must follow applicable housing laws. Where NYC’s Fair Chance Housing Law applies, a provider choosing to consider criminal history must first assess general eligibility and make a conditional offer, then follow the law’s limits and process. A corporate arrangement should not be used to bypass those protections.

Source: NYC Commission on Human Rights.

Questions

Corporate leases in NYC FAQ

Can my company rent an apartment for me in NYC?

Potentially. First confirm that the owner and building accept the company as tenant and approve the proposed occupants and lease term. Company and occupant applications may both be needed.

Is employer-paid rent the same as a corporate lease?

No. If you sign personally, you generally remain the tenant even when your employer sends the payment or reimburses you. Check the lease and your employer’s agreement.

Can I use my own LLC to lease an apartment?

Do not assume that an LLC will qualify or remove personal review. Ask the owner about entity applications, guarantees and occupancy requirements, and have your attorney review the proposed arrangement.

Are corporate leases always furnished or short-term?

No. The company’s role as leaseholder is separate from furniture, services and duration. Each belongs in the agreement.

Can another employee take over when I leave?

Only as permitted by the lease, building requirements and applicable law. Get the required approvals in writing; do not assume that paying through the same company makes a replacement automatic.
