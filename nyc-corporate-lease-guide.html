Corporate leases in NYC

Relocating for work or arranging housing for an employee? Start with who signs, who lives there and what the agreement needs to cover.

Start here

What is a corporate lease?

For a residential apartment, a corporate lease generally means a company is named as the tenant and signs the lease to house an employee or other approved occupant. The company takes on the obligations stated in the agreement; the people living there must also be identified and approved as required.

It is a housing arrangement, not an office lease. Putting a company’s name on an apartment lease does not, by itself, permit business operations, rotating guests or short-term rentals.

The first question I ask is simple: who will sign the lease, who will live there, and who will pay? Those answers guide the search.

Choose the structure

Company lease, housing allowance or guaranty?

The company signs
The business applies as the proposed tenant. Your agent can coordinate confirmation of the legal entity, authorized signer, approved occupants and any separate guaranty with the company and building.
The employee signs
The individual remains the tenant even if the employer pays the rent or reimburses it through a housing allowance. Payment support alone does not replace the tenant’s obligations.
The employer guarantees
The employee signs the lease and the company gives a separate, landlord-approved guaranty. Its scope depends on the written terms.

“Corporate housing” may instead describe a furnished accommodation service. Your agent can help clarify the operator, contracting party and included services, while your company and attorney review the agreement and cancellation terms. The label alone does not answer those questions.

Source: New York Attorney General’s explanation of lease terms.

The process

How to arrange a corporate lease in NYC.

  1. Set the brief. Work with your agent and HR or relocation team to establish the budget, dates, location, furnishings, occupants and proposed leaseholder. Include utilities and other recurring costs in the brief.
  2. Confirm acceptance before touring. Have your agent ask whether the owner and building permit the proposed corporate arrangement, lease length and use before scheduling tours. For a condo or co-op, your agent can also confirm management’s application and approval process.
  3. Get the application requirements in writing. A corporate application may differ from an individual one. Your agent can obtain the requirements from management and coordinate with the company and occupant so each knows what to provide.
  4. Review the agreement. Have the appropriate company signatory and, where needed, your attorney review liability, occupancy, renewal and early-exit terms before signing.
  5. Coordinate the move. Your agent can coordinate with management on approval, insurance requirements, move-in reservations and key collection. The company or tenant should verify payment instructions directly with the authorized recipient before sending funds.

As a local example, UDR’s 10 Hanover Square publishes a separate company application process and business-credit requirements. That is one building’s policy, not a universal NYC standard.

Source: 10 Hanover Square’s corporate-housing FAQ.

A short preparation checklist

Your agent can ask management which items apply to your arrangement:

  • Company legal name, registered address and formation information.
  • Evidence that the proposed signer can bind the company.
  • Company financial information, business-credit information or references requested for review.
  • Approved occupant details and the building’s individual application requirements.
  • Any proposed guaranty, insurance documentation and the company contact responsible for payments.

This is a preparation list, not a fixed application standard. A company lease does not automatically remove review of the individual occupant.

A more focused search

How an agent makes the process easier.

An apartment being available for rent does not mean the owner will accept a company as the tenant. Corporate-lease policies add another filter to the search, alongside budget, location and timing. Checking that fit early helps avoid tours and applications that cannot move forward.

Find a workable shortlist

An agent familiar with corporate relocations can draw on building contacts and previous application experience, then verify current policies for your specific company, occupants and lease term.

Ask the right questions early

Your agent can clarify company screening, guaranty requirements, furnished options and whether a change of employee can be accommodated. If a policy is strict, the goal is to establish what is genuinely possible before you commit.

Keep the application moving

Coordinating HR, the company signatory, the occupant and building management can involve several handoffs. Your agent can organize requirements and follow up on outstanding items.

Make the move easier

From showing appointments to board or management submissions and move-in logistics, a clear point of contact helps keep the process organized. Your attorney handles legal review; the owner or building makes the approval decision.

Before you sign

What if your work plans change?

A work assignment can change before the lease ends. Your agent can raise that possibility with the owner while terms are being negotiated, so your company and attorney can assess the options before signing.

Who can live there?

Your agent can ask whether the owner will allow a replacement employee, what screening is required and how approval is documented. The lease and building rules determine the available flexibility.

What if plans change?

Your agent can raise notice, renewal and early-exit needs with the owner; your company and attorney should review any proposed termination, assignment or subletting terms. An employer transfer does not automatically release the tenant from the lease.

Who owes what?

Your agent can help obtain a clear breakdown of rent, utilities and included services. Your company and attorney should confirm damage responsibility, insurance, any guaranty and the employee’s separate agreement with the employer.

What is included?

For furnished housing, your agent can request a written inventory and confirm internet, utilities, cleaning and move-out arrangements with the provider.

These are discussion points for your lease review. If you need an attorney introduction, I can help connect you.

Budget clearly

What does a corporate lease cost?

There is no single NYC corporate-lease price or standard premium. Your agent can help compare the rent, lease term, furnishings, included services and itemized charges across suitable options. Some operators publish corporate premiums, but their policies are not a rule for every building.

Source: UDR’s property-specific terms.

Deposits and application charges

Deposits: New York generally caps a covered residential deposit or advance at one month’s rent, subject to statutory exceptions. A company lease should not be treated as an automatic exemption. Your agent can obtain the proposed charges for your company and attorney to review.

Source: New York General Obligations Law § 7-108.

Application charges: the Attorney General describes a $20 limit for residential credit and background checks. Company applications and condo or co-op charges can raise different questions; have your agent request an itemized schedule and the basis for each charge, with legal questions reviewed by your attorney.

Source: New York Attorney General’s tenant guide.

Length of stay

A corporate lease is not a short-term rental workaround.

NYC generally prohibits renting an entire ordinary apartment or home for fewer than 30 days. Calling the booking “corporate housing” does not create an exception. For a shorter assignment, your agent or relocation team can help identify lawful hotel or extended-stay accommodation.

A stay of 30 days or more still needs to comply with the lease, building rules and permitted use. Duration alone does not establish that a particular arrangement is allowed.

Source: NYC Office of Special Enforcement.

A note on screening

Company and occupant review must follow applicable housing laws. Where NYC’s Fair Chance Housing Law applies, a provider choosing to consider criminal history must first assess general eligibility and make a conditional offer, then follow the law’s limits and process. A corporate arrangement should not be used to bypass those protections.

Source: NYC Commission on Human Rights.

Questions

Corporate leases in NYC FAQ

Can my company rent an apartment for me in NYC?

Potentially. Your agent can first confirm that the owner and building accept the company as tenant and approve the proposed occupants and lease term. Company and occupant applications may both be needed.

Is employer-paid rent the same as a corporate lease?

No. If you sign personally, you generally remain the tenant even when your employer sends the payment or reimburses you. Your company and attorney can clarify how the lease and employer’s agreement allocate responsibility.

Can I use my own LLC to lease an apartment?

An LLC may be considered, but acceptance and personal review requirements vary. Your agent can ask the owner about entity applications, guarantees and occupancy requirements, and your attorney can review the proposed arrangement.

Are corporate leases always furnished or short-term?

No. The company’s role as leaseholder is separate from furniture, services and duration. Each belongs in the agreement.

Can another employee take over when I leave?

It may be possible. Your agent can ask whether the owner allows a change of approved occupant and coordinate any new application or written consent. Replacing an occupant is different from transferring the lease itself. The lease, building rules and applicable law govern what is allowed; keeping the same company as tenant does not automatically authorize a different employee to move in.
