Chelsea is on Manhattan's West Side, running north from 14th Street between Sixth Avenue and the Hudson River. It mixes 19th-century rowhouses in a landmarked historic district, large prewar apartment complexes, converted lofts and new condo buildings along the High Line. This guide covers where Chelsea begins and ends, what kinds of apartments you'll find, how to get around, and what the market looks like now. Glenn Velasco is a Licensed Real Estate Salesperson at Compass who works with buyers, sellers and renters in Chelsea.

01 / Boundaries

Where is Chelsea? Boundaries and ZIP codes

Chelsea's boundaries are roughly 14th Street on the south, Sixth Avenue on the east and the Hudson River and West Street on the west. Its northern edge is described as anywhere from the upper 20s to 34th Street. The part west of Tenth Avenue is often called West Chelsea. Neighborhood boundaries in New York City aren't officially set, so listing sites may draw them differently.

Chelsea is in Manhattan Community Districts 4 and 5. Its primary ZIP codes are 10011 and 10001.

02 / History

A short history: the Clarke estate to the High Line

Chelsea takes its name from the estate of Major Thomas Clarke, who bought the land in 1750. In 1827 Clement Clarke Moore, who inherited the estate, gave the land for the General Theological Seminary. The rowhouse blocks that grew up around it became the Chelsea Historic District, designated by the Landmarks Preservation Commission in 1970 and extended in 1981. Industry grew along the Hudson; the Chelsea Piers were the city's main ocean liner terminal from 1910 to 1935, and the former Nabisco factory now houses Chelsea Market. From the late 1990s, galleries moved into West Chelsea, and rezoning alongside the High Line brought a wave of new construction.

03 / Housing

Chelsea apartments: co-ops, condos and rentals

Rowhouses and the historic district

The side streets of the historic district are lined with 19th-century rowhouses, some still single-family townhouses and many divided into apartments. Exterior changes inside the district need Landmarks approval, which is worth knowing before you plan a renovation.

Prewar complexes and co-ops

Chelsea has some of the city's large apartment complexes. London Terrace was one of the world's largest apartment blocks when it opened in 1930. Penn South is a cooperative development from 1962. Co-ops require board approval and a full financial package, and many limit financing, subletting or pied-à-terre use. If you're buying from abroad, condos are usually the simpler route, because co-op boards often ask for U.S. financial history. Glenn can walk you through the difference in English, Spanish, Italian or French.

Condos, lofts and rentals

Condos range from converted industrial loft buildings to new towers near the High Line and Hudson River Park. Rentals include rental buildings, walk-ups and condo apartments rented by their owners, where the building's own rules apply.

05 / Subway lines

Getting around: Chelsea subway lines

Chelsea is served by these stations, with the closest stop depending on the block:

  • 14 St (A, C, E) and 8 Av (L), at Eighth Avenue
  • 14 St (1, 2, 3), at Seventh Avenue
  • 14 St (F, M), at Sixth Avenue
  • 18 St, 23 St and 28 St (1), on Seventh Avenue
  • 23 St (C, E), on Eighth Avenue
  • 23 St (F, M), on Sixth Avenue
  • 34 St–Hudson Yards (7), which opened in September 2015 with its main entrance in Chelsea

West Chelsea, near Tenth and Eleventh Avenues, is a longer walk from most stations.

06 / Parks, galleries and landmarks

Parks, galleries and landmarks

The High Line, a park built on an elevated rail line, runs through West Chelsea from Gansevoort Street north toward Hudson Yards; its first section opened in 2009. Hudson River Park runs along the waterfront, next to Chelsea Piers. West Chelsea between Tenth and Eleventh Avenues and 16th and 28th Streets has more than 200 art galleries. The New York Public Library's Muhlenberg Library, at 209 West 23rd Street, opened in 1906.

10 / FAQ

Chelsea FAQ

What are the boundaries of Chelsea in Manhattan?

Chelsea runs roughly from 14th Street north to the upper 20s or 34th Street, between Sixth Avenue and the Hudson River. The part west of Tenth Avenue is often called West Chelsea. Neighborhood boundaries in New York City aren't officially set, so other sources may differ.

What ZIP code is Chelsea NYC?

Chelsea's primary ZIP codes are 10011 and 10001. It is in Manhattan Community Districts 4 and 5.

What subway lines serve Chelsea?

The A, C and E run under Eighth Avenue, the 1, 2 and 3 under Seventh Avenue and the F and M under Sixth Avenue, with the L along 14th Street and the 7 at 34th Street–Hudson Yards.

What is the average rent in Chelsea?

StreetEasy doesn't publish an average, but its median asking rent for Chelsea was $6,295 a month in September 2026, up from $5,995 in April. Half of the listed apartments asked more and half asked less, and studios and larger apartments sit far apart, so the right number depends on the size and building you want.

How much does it cost to live in Chelsea?

StreetEasy's September 2026 data shows a median asking price of $1,799,999 and a median asking rent of $6,295 a month. Redfin reports a median sale price of $1,694,525 for the three months ending September 2026. The median asking rent is the closest public measure of the average rent in Chelsea. Prices vary widely by building and apartment, so ask Glenn for recent comparable sales.

What is the High Line?

The High Line is a public park built on a former elevated rail line on Manhattan's West Side. It runs through West Chelsea, and its first section opened in 2009.
