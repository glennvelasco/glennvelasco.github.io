NYC Guarantor Requirements

Moving to NYC, or renting your first apartment here? If you’re short of the 40x income rule, a guarantor can get your lease approved. Here’s how the 70x–80x rule works, what a guarantor provides, what to do without one and what New York law lets a landlord collect upfront.

By Glenn Velasco
Licensed Real Estate Salesperson · Compass

01 / The basics

What is a guarantor for an NYC apartment?

A guarantor is a person who signs on to your lease and agrees to cover the rent if you don’t pay it. In most cases, the guarantor is a parent or close relative. Landlords ask for one when your income, credit or rental history doesn’t meet their requirements on its own. (CityRealty)

Who typically needs one? Students and recent graduates with short work histories, people new to the US with no US credit history, and anyone whose income falls below the landlord’s rent-to-income rule. (CityRealty)

A guarantor is a screening requirement set by each landlord, not a legal requirement. Every building sets its own rules, so I confirm them in writing before you apply. (Brick Underground)

02 / The numbers

NYC rental requirements: the 40x and 70x–80x rules

Most NYC landlords use two income tests:

  • The 40x rent rule (tenant): your annual income should be at least 40 times the monthly rent.
  • The 70x–80x rule (guarantor): if you don’t meet 40x, your guarantor’s annual income should typically be 70 to 80 times the monthly rent (70x–80x), depending on the landlord.

Here’s how it works on a $3,000-a-month apartment:

RuleAnnual income needed
Tenant40x monthly rent$120,000
Guarantor70x–80x monthly rent$210,000–$240,000

These are landlord policies, not laws, and they vary. Some management companies publish exactly these thresholds (The Feil Organization, GPG Management). Others also accept liquid assets instead of income; one landlord, for example, accepts guarantors with assets of 6 times the annual rent (Marquis Apartments). If you’re sharing with roommates, some owners will combine guarantors’ incomes and others accept only one guarantor. (CityRealty)

03 / The paperwork

What a guarantor needs to provide

A guarantor applies just like you do. Expect the landlord to ask for:

  • A photo ID
  • Recent pay stubs or other proof of income
  • One to two years of tax returns
  • Recent bank statements
  • Their information so the landlord can run a credit check

(CityRealty, GPG Management)

In New York, the most a landlord can charge for a credit and background check is $20. The landlord must give the applicant a copy of the check. Applicants can also supply their own credit or background check, as long as it was run in the past 30 days. (New York Attorney General)

Have your guarantor gather the documents before you start touring. When you find the right apartment, both applications go in together.

04 / The alternative

Guarantor services: institutional guarantors in NYC

Don’t have a parent or relative who meets 70x–80x? An institutional guarantor, also called a guarantor service or guarantor company, may be able to stand in. You pay the company a fee, and the company guarantees your lease to the landlord. (CityRealty)

Guarantor services set their own qualifying rules, which are usually lower than a personal guarantor’s 70x–80x. For example, Insurent lists a minimum annual income of 27.5 times the monthly rent, or liquid assets of at least 50 times the monthly rent. (Insurent)

Two companies that operate in New York, as each describes itself:

  • Insurent issues lease guarantees for US and non-US renters. It says it does not require US credit history for non-US applicants, and it lists its own income and asset minimums. It also publishes its average fee ranges, stated as a percentage of one month’s rent. (Insurent)
  • Insurent also says that if you pay its fee and don’t get the apartment, it refunds the fee in full. (Insurent)
  • TheGuarantors sells a Lease Guarantee policy that pays the landlord if you miss rent. You must then repay TheGuarantors. It says applying is free and the premium is paid upfront and isn’t refundable. (TheGuarantors, TheGuarantors FAQ)
  • TheGuarantors says its policy covers the landlord for the term of your lease. The premium isn’t a deposit, it isn’t returned when you move out and it doesn’t reduce what you’d owe if it pays a claim. Its policy can also include Deposit Coverage, an alternative to a security deposit, if the landlord chooses. At some buildings you can only apply through the building’s invitation. (TheGuarantors FAQ)

Before you apply, check four things:

  1. Does the building accept the company? Not every landlord works with every service. (CityRealty)
  2. What does it cost for your lease? Get the quote in writing. It’s based on the rent, the lease length and your profile.
  3. What happens if you miss rent? A guarantee protects the landlord, not you. You still owe the rent.
  4. What’s refundable? Ask what happens to the fee if your application is turned down, and whether coverage lasts for the whole lease.

I don’t endorse a particular company. I’ll tell you which services a building accepts so you apply to the right one.

05 / Beyond New York

Moving to NYC from out of state or abroad

Out-of-state guarantors. No single rule covers this. Some NYC landlords accept guarantors who live anywhere in the tri-state area, some prefer New York residents, and some approve guarantors from other states if they provide full financial documents. Landlords that limit guarantors to the area say it’s easier to collect if a tenant defaults. (Brick Underground)

International guarantors. A guarantor who lives in another country is harder to get approved. Many owners won’t accept foreign guarantors. Those that do often ask for higher income and more money upfront. (CityRealty)

International students and new arrivals. Institutional guarantors are the usual route when you have no US credit history. Insurent says it can qualify an international student through a parent or relative overseas, who signs only Insurent’s agreement, not the lease. (Insurent) TheGuarantors says it approves renters who are not US citizens or green card holders, renters with no Social Security number and renters with no credit score, though approval isn’t guaranteed. (TheGuarantors)

Moving for a new job. If you haven’t started yet, have your offer letter ready. Insurent accepts a letter from your new employer as proof of income. (Insurent) Landlords may ask non-US citizens for proof of employment, a work visa and/or a green card. (GPG Management)

Corporate leases. If your employer is signing the lease, both companies say they cover corporate leases or US corporations. (Insurent, TheGuarantors FAQ)

In New York City, landlords may not discriminate against tenants based on immigration status. (New York Attorney General)

06 / Your options

How to rent in NYC without a guarantor

  1. Qualify on your own. If your income meets 40x, you usually don’t need a guarantor. Some landlords also accept liquid assets. (Marquis Apartments)
  2. Use an institutional guarantor at a building that accepts one (see section 04).
  3. Adjust the rent to the rule. 40x works backward: on $100,000 of income, the rent that fits is $2,500 a month. I’ll set your search to the rent you qualify for, so you only apply where you can win.
  4. Prepaying rent isn’t an option. New York law doesn’t let a landlord ask for or accept months of rent in advance (see section 07), so it can’t replace a guarantor.

07 / The money

Upfront costs: what NY law lets a landlord collect

The 2019 Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act (HSTPA) set statewide limits on what you can be asked to pay before you move in.

Security deposit: one month’s rent, at most. The law covers unregulated apartments under General Obligations Law § 7-108, and rent-stabilized apartments too. The cap also applies to money from your guarantor or anyone else paying on your behalf. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108, NYS Homes and Community Renewal, Fact Sheet #9)

Advance rent counts toward the same cap. The statute says: No deposit or advance shall exceed the amount of one month’s rent. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108) In practice, that means:

  • A landlord can’t ask for last month’s rent on top of a security deposit. (New York Attorney General)
  • The cap applies however long the lease is: a longer lease doesn’t let a landlord collect more upfront. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108)
  • Rent-stabilized landlords can’t charge a fee or deposit to reserve an apartment. HCR calls this “key money.” (NYS Homes and Community Renewal, Fact Sheet #9)

A full year upfront isn’t allowed. A landlord can’t ask for it or accept it, even if you offer. The statute caps any deposit or advance at one month’s rent, and it makes void any agreement in which a tenant waives these rights. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108) At signing, the most a landlord can collect in rent and deposit is the first month’s rent plus a security deposit of no more than one month’s rent. If a landlord asks for more, talk to a lawyer.

Exceptions. The statute’s one-month cap doesn’t apply to registered seasonal-use rentals or owner-occupied co-op apartments, which follow their own rules. Rent-controlled apartments are covered by separate regulations, which also limit the deposit to one month. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108, NYS Homes and Community Renewal, Fact Sheet #9)

Application fees. The most a landlord can charge for a credit and background check is $20. (New York Attorney General) Some landlords charge it to guarantors too. (The Feil Organization)

Getting the deposit back. For unregulated apartments, the landlord has 14 days after you move out to return the deposit, with an itemized statement of anything kept. (New York Attorney General) A landlord who willfully breaks these rules can owe up to twice the deposit or advance. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108)

08 / The law

A note on the FARE Act

New York City’s FARE Act took effect on June 11, 2025. Brokers who represent the landlord, including listing agents, can’t charge you a broker fee. Landlords must disclose in writing every fee you’ll pay before you sign. Renters can still choose to hire and pay their own broker. (NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection)

The law doesn’t change guarantor rules. A guarantor company’s fee is something you pay the company, not a fee the landlord charges you.

09 / FAQ

NYC guarantor FAQ

What is a guarantor in NYC?

A person, usually a parent or close relative, who signs on to your lease and agrees to pay the rent if you don’t. Landlords ask for one when you don’t meet their income or credit requirements. (CityRealty)

How much does a guarantor need to make in NYC?

Most landlords ask for annual income of 70 to 80 times the monthly rent. On a $3,000 apartment, that’s $210,000 to $240,000 a year. It’s a landlord policy, not a law, so it varies by building. (CityRealty)

How much income do you need to rent an apartment in NYC?

Many landlords use the 40x rule: your annual income should be at least 40 times the monthly rent. On a $3,000 apartment, that’s $120,000 a year. If you fall short, they typically ask for a guarantor. (CityRealty)

I’m moving to NYC from out of state. Can my guarantor live outside New York?

It depends on the landlord. Some accept tri-state guarantors, some want New York residents and some accept guarantors from other states with full financial documents. (Brick Underground) A guarantor outside the US is accepted less often. (CityRealty)

What is a guarantor service?

A company, such as Insurent or TheGuarantors, that guarantees your lease to the landlord for a fee. The building has to accept the company, and you still owe the rent. (Insurent, TheGuarantors)

Can a landlord accept rent upfront instead of a guarantor?

No. New York law caps any deposit or advance at one month’s rent, so a landlord can’t ask for or accept a year of rent, or last month’s rent, upfront, even if you offer. At signing, the most a landlord can collect in rent and deposit is the first month’s rent plus a security deposit of no more than one month’s rent. (N.Y. General Obligations Law § 7-108) To rent without a guarantor, qualify on your own income or use a guarantor service.

How much can a landlord charge for a guarantor’s application?

In New York, the most a landlord can charge for a credit and background check is $20, and the applicant must get a copy. (New York Attorney General) Landlords like The Feil Organization charge that fee per applicant, including guarantors.
