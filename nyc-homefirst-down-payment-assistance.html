NYC HomeFirst: Down Payment Assistance Guide

Could HomeFirst help with your down payment? A practical starting point for understanding eligibility, the upfront budget and the steps toward approval.

By Glenn Velasco
Licensed Real Estate Salesperson · Compass

Published November 3, 2026 · Official sources checked the same day.

In this guide
  1. What HomeFirst offers
  2. Eligibility and income
  3. Eligible homes
  4. Your cash contribution
  5. Occupancy and repayment
  6. How to start

The essentials

Help with the upfront cost of buying.

NYC HomeFirst can help eligible first-time buyers with a down payment or closing costs on a home in any of the five boroughs. Condos, co-ops and one- to four-family homes can qualify. The program is administered by NYC’s Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD). Official program overview.

The assistance is a 0% interest, forgivable loan, capped at the lower of $100,000 or 20% of the purchase price. Forgiveness depends on meeting the loan conditions; the maximum is not an automatic award. HPD term sheet.

Before you search

Start with an approved housing counselor.

An HPD-approved counseling agency is the starting point for checking eligibility and completing the required homebuyer education. Use the city’s current agency directory, which also identifies available languages.

  • You generally must not have owned a home during the preceding three years.
  • The home must be your primary residence.
  • You must contribute at least 3% of the price from your own funds.
  • You need a qualifying mortgage from a participating lender. The HomeFirst term sheet excludes mortgage brokers.

Your counselor and lender must also assess income, assets, credit, the property and the full application. Borrower and mortgage requirements.

Current household income limits

HPD publishes these maximum household incomes at 120% of area median income (AMI), effective June 1, 2026. Household size matters; being under the limit alone does not establish eligibility.

The property matters

Confirm the home fits before making an offer.

Price limits depend on location, property type and whether the home is existing, newly built or rehabilitated. Ask your counselor to confirm the applicable cap for the specific address. The home must also pass the required inspection. HPD property requirements and price-limit links.

I can help you compare homes within the confirmed budget. Bring a New York real estate attorney into the process early to review the contract, building documents and assistance conditions.

Your cash contribution

Assistance is one part of the budget.

For an eligible $500,000 purchase, 3% is $15,000 of your own funds; the program cap would be $100,000. This illustrates the rules, not an approval or a complete cash-to-close estimate.

The published term sheet also lists a $1,650 processing fee: $250 at application submission and $1,400 at closing. Confirm current charges with the servicer. Loan terms and fees.

Review your mortgage, closing costs and remaining reserves together. Our buyer guide’s closing-cost breakdown explains the broader budget.

The longer-term commitment

Understand the conditions for forgiveness.

The occupancy period is generally 10 years for loans of $40,000 or less and 15 years for larger loans. City-funded loans require 15 years regardless of amount. Selling, transferring ownership, moving out or refinancing can trigger repayment obligations. Have your attorney review the actual loan documents. HPD repayment and recapture terms.

Where to begin

Your first conversation is with a counselor.

  1. Contact an approved agency. Ask about your household, available funds and the education process.
  2. Work with a participating lender. Confirm the mortgage path before setting your search budget.
  3. Assemble the team and find an eligible home. Coordinate your agent, attorney, counselor and lender.
  4. Complete the application and closing conditions. Obtain the required inspection and confirm approval and funding before relying on assistance.

Use the official program brochure, participating-lender list and application checklist. The checklist covers household, financial, contract, mortgage and property records; your counselor will explain which apply.

Your next step
