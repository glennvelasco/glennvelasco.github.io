Buyer’s Guide

Buying a home is one of the most emotionally and financially significant decisions most people will make in their lifetime. With my industry knowledge, I am prepared to guide you through this often complex journey and make your experience as pleasant as possible.

  1. 01

    Step 01 of 11

    Find an agent

    As your agent, I know the neighborhoods you’re considering and can help guide your search. I share real-time market insights on comparable homes and use my knowledge of on and off-market listings to find homes that meet your criteria.

  2. 02

    Step 02 of 11

    Get pre-approved

    Before beginning your search, your first step is to get pre-approved for a mortgage loan (unless you will be paying in cash for the full price of your home). Based on your income and credit history, the mortgage broker will determine how much the bank will lend you, which will help you determine the price range for your search. I can connect you to a mortgage broker.

  3. 03

    Step 03 of 11

    Visit properties

    Attend viewings and open houses spanning a range of areas and property types. Now is the time to consider your ideal home’s location and amenities. Remember your home doesn’t exist in a vacuum: noise level, amenities and what’s in the neighborhood are as important to your happiness as the inside of your home.

  4. 04

    Step 04 of 11

    Co-op or condo

    Those who buy condos receive deeds and specified pieces of real estate, while those who buy co-ops maintain shares of corporations that own the buildings where their units are. Condos are typically more expensive from purchase to closing but provide more flexibility to the owner.

  5. 05

    Step 05 of 11

    Negotiate

    Reach an agreement with the seller on price and terms. Your “offer” is a non-binding agreement to pay a certain price for the home; if it is lower than the list price, the seller will likely return with a “counter offer,” which you can accept, reject, or answer with another offer. I will provide advice on pricing throughout.

  6. 06

    Step 06 of 11

    Review

    Analyze the contract of sale, building financials, and board minutes with your attorney. It’s best to work with an attorney who specializes in New York City co-op and condo sales.

  7. 07

    Step 07 of 11

    Sign the contract

    Sign the contract and put down your deposit. Then complete your loan application and organize an appraisal with your bank.

  8. 08

    Step 08 of 11

    Prepare your board package

    Co-ops are run by a board of directors, who will require a board package: financial documents including tax returns, bank statements, mortgage commitment from your bank, and letter of reference. I will help prepare your board package as well as prepare you for your interview. Condos do not generally require board packages or interviews, but you will need a purchase application that shows you are financially qualified. Buying a townhouse? Skip this step.

  9. 09

    Step 09 of 11

    Closing costs

    Plan for closing costs on top of your price: attorney fees (~$2,500 - $5,000+), bank and appraisal fees, and building fees such as the board package ($500 - $2,000). Purchases of $1M or more also pay the Mansion Tax, from 1.00% ($1M to < $2M) up to 3.90% ($25M or more). Ask me for the full 2025 breakdown for condos, co-ops and townhouses.

  10. 10

    Step 10 of 11

    Approval and closing

    The board approves the purchase, the bank issues clearance to close, and the closing is scheduled by the attorneys. Before you sign, you will do a final walk-through.

  11. 11

    Step 11 of 11

    Welcome home

    Congratulations! You are now a homeowner.
