Buying a home in New York City

A practical guide to condos, co-ops, budgeting, documents and closing—for first-time buyers, experienced owners and people moving from abroad.

01 / Before you start

Start with how you will use the home.

Buying in New York means evaluating both an apartment and the building around it. Before touring, decide whether this will be your main home, a second home or a rental property. That choice shapes the questions we ask about use, financing and building rules.

Then define a comfortable monthly budget, available purchase funds and the cash you want to keep afterward. We can build a search around your daily travel, space, light, elevator needs and timing using the NYC neighborhood guides.

My role is to help you compare homes, understand pricing and coordinate the search and transaction. Your attorney, lender and other specialists handle the legal, lending and technical decisions in their fields.

02 / Condo or co-op?

Condo or co-op: understand the ownership.

Condominium

You own the unit and a share of common areas. Budget for common charges and your unit’s property taxes, and check the rules for alterations, occupancy and rentals.

Cooperative

You own shares in the corporation that owns the building, paired with a proprietary lease. Monthly maintenance generally covers building operations and property taxes, and may include an underlying building mortgage.

Source: NYC Bar: co-ops and condos.

Compare specific buildings. Neither label guarantees a fixed approval timeline, rental flexibility or financing terms. Ask about the application, any interview, planned work, special assessments and whether your intended use is permitted.

03 / Budget and closing costs

Build a budget beyond the purchase price.

  • Purchase funds: the part of the price paid from your own money. A contract deposit counts toward the purchase; do not count it a second time when calculating your remaining balance.
  • Closing costs: request an itemized estimate covering legal, financing, tax and building charges, plus any agent compensation payable under your representation agreement.
  • Ongoing ownership: mortgage payments, building charges, applicable taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs and reserves.

A quick tax example

For an ordinary resale condo priced at $1.2 million with an $800,000 mortgage: Mansion Tax is $12,000 and the usual buyer-paid mortgage recording tax is $15,400. That is $27,400 for those two taxes alone—not total closing costs. Other fees, prepayments, adjustments and any applicable credits or exceptions still need to be calculated.

Before you commit, request one itemized cash-to-close estimate. Keep your down payment, closing costs, refundable deposits and post-closing reserves separate; subtract the contract deposit already paid when calculating the remaining amount due.

Buying your first home?

NYC’s HomeFirst program offers qualifying first-time buyers up to $100,000 toward a down payment or closing costs. Income, education, lender, property and occupancy requirements apply. Check eligibility with an approved counseling agency before treating assistance as available funds. Official HomeFirst requirements.

04 / Financing and documents

Prepare documents around your situation.

Ask the lender for its checklist before you start collecting or translating records. Salaried, self-employed and international buyers may need different documentation. Confirm how income, assets, existing debts and the source of purchase funds will be verified.

  • Which records and time periods are required?
  • How will overseas income or assets be evaluated?
  • Are translations needed, and in what format?
  • What down payment, reserves and building requirements apply?
  • How should transfers or gifts be documented?

A preapproval is a preliminary lending assessment, not a guarantee that the loan or property will be approved. Keep the lender informed of material changes.

05 / International buyers

Buying from another country.

New York law allows noncitizens to hold and transfer real property. Financing, tax treatment and building procedures still need individual review. Real Property Law §10.

If your funds are in another currency, budget for the dollars needed at each stage, including transfer costs and timing. Confirm which documents and signatures require your presence before arranging travel. Coordinate ownership structure and cross-border reporting with qualified legal and tax advisers.

For Italian buyers, the Italian guide includes Italy-specific tax and currency context. The Spanish guide explains the process for readers with a range of financing and residency situations.

For a first conversation, share your goals, approximate budget and timing. Do not send passports, bank statements or tax returns through the public website form.

06 / The purchase process

From the first search to the keys.

  1. Agree on the search and representation.

    We review priorities, services, duration and compensation under the representation agreement. If financing, speak with a lender before relying on a price range.

  2. Tour and compare.

    We assess homes against your needs, comparable sales, recurring expenses and building questions. A home that photographs well still needs a practical and financial review.

  3. Prepare an offer.

    Discuss price, payment method, timing and proposed conditions with your attorney. Agreement on terms, a contract sent out and a signed contract are distinct stages.

  4. Review before signing.

    Your attorney reviews the relevant contract and building documents. The Attorney General recommends reading the offering plan and consulting an attorney before signing. Arrange appropriate physical inspections as well. Attorney General guidance.

  5. Complete lender and building requirements.

    I help organize a required board package according to the building’s instructions. Track financing conditions, appraisals, interviews, approvals and deadlines with the relevant professionals.

  6. Review closing figures and walk through.

    For mortgages covered by the rule, you must receive the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. Compare it with your Loan Estimate and resolve unexpected changes. CFPB closing checklist and explanations.

    We coordinate a final walk-through and the remaining steps. Confirm funds, building move-in procedures and actual possession before booking the move. Verify wire instructions through a previously confirmed contact number. CFPB wire-fraud guidance.

07 / Frequently asked questions

Questions to settle before moving forward.

How long will buying take?

There is no single reliable timeline. Search, due diligence, financing and building procedures each affect timing. We build a calendar for the selected property and revise it as milestones are confirmed.

Can I rent the home out later?

Have the building’s rules and applicable law checked before relying on rental income. The intended rental length and occupancy matter; buying a condo does not automatically authorize short-term rentals.

Can I start while living outside New York?

Yes, we can begin the search remotely. Confirm with your attorney and lender which steps can be completed from where you live and which need additional arrangements.

Does a cash purchase remove the need for due diligence?

No. Paying without a mortgage does not answer questions about building finances, physical conditions, ownership documents or permitted use.

08 / Buyer glossary

Terms you will encounter.

Down payment
Your own funds applied to the price, separate from closing costs.
Board package
The documents submitted under the building’s purchase procedure.
Offering plan
Offering documentation to review with your attorney alongside relevant amendments.
Assessment
In a building’s charges, an additional contribution. Ask about amount, purpose and duration.
Closing
The final transaction stage, involving documents, payments and the transfer appropriate to your purchase.
