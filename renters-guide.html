Renter’s Guide

Apartments rent quickly in New York City. Here is the path, one step at a time.

Part 1 of 3

Search

  1. 01

    Start early

    Begin about 30–45 days before your move-in date. Most landlords want a vacant unit leased within two weeks of applying.

  2. 02

    Prepare your documents

    An employment letter, your last two pay stubs, two years of tax returns and W-2s, two recent bank statements, landlord references and a photo ID.

  3. 03

    Know the income rule

    Landlords typically ask for combined annual income of 40x–50x the monthly rent.

  4. 04

    Line up a guarantor

    If you fall short, a guarantor earning at least 70x–80x the monthly rent can back the lease. International applicants may need a US guarantor.

Part 2 of 3

Apply

  1. 05

    Apply for a rental

    The landlord reviews in 24–48 hours. Once approved, the lease is usually signed within the next 24 to 48 hours.

  2. 06

    Apply for a condo

    Both the owner and the condo board must approve. Allow a few days to several weeks, sometimes with an interview.

  3. 07

    Apply for a co-op

    Two approvals, like a condo, but allow 30 days from a complete package.

  4. 08

    Roommates and pets

    Every roommate and guarantor applies in full and signs the lease. Pet rules vary by building, so share your pet’s weight and breed.

Part 3 of 3

Sign and move in

  1. 09

    Sign and pay

    Secure the apartment by certified check or money order: first month’s rent, a security deposit of no more than one month’s rent, and the broker fee if represented.

  2. 10

    Condo and co-op fees

    Expect more: a move-in fee of $1,000, often refundable, an application fee of $250–300 and a managing agent fee of $250–400.

A broker saves you time, helps you secure a fair price and puts your application in the best light.
