The idea

What is a Compass Private Exclusive?

A Compass Private Exclusive is a way to begin marketing a home through private, one-to-one introductions before a broad public launch. It can give a seller room to explore interest while deciding when, how and where the property becomes widely visible.

Think of it as a launch option within your selling plan. It can suit a seller who values discretion or needs more preparation time. Going directly to the public market is also an option.

Source: Compass’s seller disclosure.

Why consider it

Make your first public impression more deliberate.

Protect your privacy

Limit the initial circulation of your home’s photographs and floor plan, and arrange appointments around your life.

Gather an early response

Learn what prospective buyers ask about the price, layout and presentation before expanding the launch.

Prepare while building interest

Use an eligible private phase while finishing painting, staging or other preparation, with access suited to the home’s condition.

Manage the public debut

Where the applicable listing status allows it, begin without accumulating public days on market or displaying public price reductions.

Source: Compass Private Exclusives.

The practical question is what you want to learn. We agree on that before launch: whether the asking price draws serious interest, which details need clearer presentation, and what would justify moving to a wider audience.

The NYC detail

What does “no days on market” actually mean?

Days on market is a listing-system measure, not the total time you have spent preparing or trying to sell. REBNY’s 2026 rules say that Participant Only Network and Coming Soon listings do not accrue RLS days on market while they remain in those qualifying settings.

Source: REBNY’s 2026 rule changes.

That distinction matters: a private period can support a more deliberate public debut, but it does not make elapsed time disappear or erase an earlier listing history. I confirm the actual status and distribution plan before describing what buyers will see.

How the NYC listing options differ

Owner Opt Out: no RLS distribution or public marketing; a signed owner form is required. Personalized one-to-one co-broking is permitted, and days on market do not accrue.

Participant Only: available to authorized RLS participants, with no public or mass marketing. Days on market do not accrue.

RLS Coming Soon: a separate, owner-authorized status limited to 14 days, with no public showings and no days-on-market accrual. A Compass marketing phase and an RLS status are not interchangeable labels.

Source: REBNY’s marketing-options guidance.

Price with purpose

Test the response, then make a decision.

A useful pricing conversation begins with comparable sales, the homes competing with yours and the features that set your property apart. Early feedback adds context; it does not replace that analysis.

We set a review point before starting. Look at substantive inquiries, showing feedback and the strength of any offers. Decide together whether to maintain the price, improve the presentation, adjust the asking price or broaden exposure.

A small audience gives you a limited sample. Little activity does not prove that no buyer will pay your price, and one enthusiastic response does not establish market value. The aim is to make the next decision better informed.

For the wider process, see the NYC seller’s guide, or start with a home valuation.

Your launch plan

Private Exclusive, Coming Soon or a public launch?

Compass describes three marketing phases: private introductions, a Coming Soon phase with broader visibility, and distribution through the applicable listing service and participating public websites. The available sequence depends on the seller’s instructions and local requirements.

Source: Compass’s three-phase disclosure.

We choose the route around your priorities. A privacy-sensitive sale may call for a limited initial audience. A home that is ready now, with an owner seeking the widest immediate competition, may be better served by a direct public launch.

Make an informed choice

More control, with tradeoffs to understand.

Private marketing gives you more control over your launch. A smaller audience can mean fewer showings and offers, and it may affect the eventual sale price. Carrying costs and the commitments in your listing agreement also remain.

Compass’s own disclosure identifies those exposure tradeoffs and allows sellers to instruct a move to the MLS. We review the current written options before choosing a plan.

Source: Compass’s seller disclosure.

Questions

Compass Private Exclusives FAQ

Does a Compass Private Exclusive guarantee a higher price?

No. It can create an opportunity for early feedback and a more controlled launch, but it cannot guarantee your sale price, timing or number of offers.

Do I have to sell privately before listing publicly?

No. Your marketing plan should reflect your priorities and the applicable rules. A direct public launch remains an option.

Is testing the market risk-free?

No. Avoiding public days-on-market history does not eliminate carrying costs, limited exposure or the possibility of a weaker response. The benefits and tradeoffs belong in the same decision.

Can buyers with another agent be considered?

Yes. Private marketing should not be presented as a requirement to use Glenn as the buyer’s agent. REBNY permits personalized one-to-one co-broking for Owner Opt Out listings, subject to the applicable rules.
