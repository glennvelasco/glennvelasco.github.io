Neighborhoods

Where these areas are, how to get around, and homes Glenn has worked on in each.

01 · Manhattan

TriBeCa

The name is short for Triangle Below Canal Street. The neighborhood runs roughly from Canal Street south to Chambers Street, between Broadway and the Hudson River. Much of it is made up of 19th- and early-20th-century warehouse and cast-iron loft buildings, many now converted to homes, and large parts sit within four historic districts designated by the NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission. Hudson River Park runs along its western edge.

Subway
1 at Franklin St, Canal St and Chambers St; A, C, E at Canal St and Chambers St; 2, 3 at Chambers St.

02 · Manhattan · incl. Lenox Hill

Upper East Side

The Upper East Side runs from about 59th Street to 96th Street, between Central Park and the East River. Museum Mile on Fifth Avenue, along the park, includes the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Lenox Hill is the area's southern section, roughly 60th to 77th Streets. Carl Schurz Park and John Jay Park sit along the East River.

4, 5, 6 on Lexington Avenue; Q on Second Avenue at 72 St, 86 St and 96 St; F at Lexington Av/63 St.

03 · Manhattan

Harlem

Harlem covers much of northern Manhattan, generally from 110th Street, at the top of Central Park, north to about 155th Street. Many blocks are lined with 19th-century brownstone and row houses. 125th Street is the main commercial street and home to the Apollo Theater. Marcus Garvey Park and the north end of Central Park are close by.

Subway
2, 3 on Lenox Avenue; A, B, C, D on St. Nicholas and Eighth Avenues; 4, 5, 6 on Lexington Avenue; 1 on Broadway.

04 · Brooklyn

Boerum Hill

Boerum Hill sits between Downtown Brooklyn, Cobble Hill and Gowanus, with Atlantic Avenue running along it. Its side streets are lined with 19th-century rowhouses, and part of the neighborhood is protected as the Boerum Hill Historic District, designated in 1973.

A, C, G at Hoyt-Schermerhorn; F, G at Bergen St; 2, 3, 4, 5 at Nevins St; Atlantic Av-Barclays Ctr is nearby.
